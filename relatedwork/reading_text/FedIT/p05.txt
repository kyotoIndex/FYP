Databricks-dolly-15k dataset into ten shards using a widely adopted partitioning method [30, 36, 94],
with each shard assigned to an individual client. The category distribution for each client’s instruction
dataset is illustrated in Figure 2a. As is evident in the figure, each user’s dataset contains imbalanced
categories of instructions, with some categories absent entirely. This reflects real-world scenarios
where users may not possess expertise across all instruction categories. In the absence of FedIT, due
to the challenges associated with collecting sensitive instruction data, the model can only be trained
on the local instruction dataset of each user, as depicted in the left subfigure of Figure 2b. However,
by implementing our FedIT approach, the model can be trained on the local instruction datasets of all
clients, as illustrated in the right subfigure of Figure 2b. As a result, FedIT allows for instruction
tuning on a dataset with enhanced diversity, and a larger number of data points, encompassing
the complete Databricks-dolly-15k dataset. The distribution of this dataset is shown in the right
subfigure of Figure 2b. Comprising eight unique categories with varying numbers of instructions,
this dataset offers increased diversity, allowing the model to be more generalized and applicable to a
wider array of tasks compared to training solely on each client’s local instruction dataset with limited
categories and quantity.
